How it holds together. Six instruments share one pulse clock: a continuous low drone, a hand-drum-style percussion kit plus a four-on-the-floor kick, an arpeggiated bass, a bowed/breath lead voice, a slow pad, and a plucked ostinato. There are three unlabeled tonal/rhythmic states (I, II, III — different scale, meter, and feel), and every 4 bars a Markov chain re-rolls a shared state, biased by the I / II / III weights you set and toward whichever state it was already in. Spread sets how often each individual layer breaks off and rolls its own state instead of following the shared one — at 0 the whole band moves together; turned up, layers can sit in different states and meters at the same time. Nothing here runs on a timer: energy is a fader you set directly (with a little continuous drift built in, so it never sits perfectly still), and each layer below has its own volume, mute, density, and a lock so you can pin it to I, II, III, or leave it on Auto to keep following the chain. The kick now leans four-on-the-floor as energy rises, hi-hats fade in above it, and the bass/pad/drone/lead all get a harder sidechain pump on every kick; past roughly 50% energy the pluck swaps to a detuned supersaw stab. Play it as long as you like — you're conducting it, not pressing play on it.
